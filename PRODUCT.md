# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Expecting parents and partners choosing a baby name together, often on mobile and in short sessions.

## Product Purpose

LeBabyName turns a long shortlist into quick head-to-head choices until one favorite remains. Partners can replay the same list and compare results.

## Positioning

The product combines official national name rankings with a simple knockout duel and private partner comparison. It is free forever, with no freemium tier or paywall.

## Operating Context

People browse official or custom name lists, complete a tournament, share a result or invite, and compare outcomes. Public name and list pages help people discover names before starting a duel.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Next.js, Auth.js with Google, PostgreSQL, Docker/Coolify, and automatic deployment from `main`.
- Six locales: French, UK English, US English, Spanish, Portuguese, and Italian.
- Official per-country datasets for France, the UK, the US, Spain, Portugal, and Italy.
- Public name facts must be sourced; unknown meanings or origins are omitted rather than inferred.
- Advertising is disabled by default and may load only after consent.
- Monetization must remain light and must never block the duel.

## Brand Commitments

LeBabyName is warm, direct, playful, and practical. Existing heart mark, cream/rose/sky palette, generous type, and plain-language product voice remain authoritative.

## Evidence on Hand

Official ranking sources and coverage notes live in `data/name-datasets.json`. No testimonials, commercial performance claims, or comprehensive etymology source is available and none may be fabricated.

## Product Principles

- Keep the core product free and immediately useful.
- Make every choice fast, clear, and comfortable on mobile.
- Use transparent, attributable name data.
- Turn sharing into a useful invitation, not pressure.
- Keep monetization subordinate to the naming experience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

All primary actions need keyboard access, visible focus, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion support, and clear labels. Gender-based lists describe the source rankings and do not claim that a name can only be used by one gender.
